## Problem

Product launches, price changes, and rebranding are high-stakes bets. Elasticity is calculated from historical data that cannot describe what is new; competitor reactions are not modeled at all; cannibalization of your own product lines only surfaces after launch. As a result, the "cost of failure" shows up in a loss report a quarter later.

**The consumer reaction forecast** gives you a scenario picture before you make your move: you see sales corridors, customer reactions, and competitor responses — while the parameters can still be changed for free.

## What this product is

A predictive system for commercial decisions:
- **"What if" scenarios**: raise/lower the price by X%, launch product Y, change packaging, rebrand, enter a new channel
- **Reaction corridors**: volumes, revenue, churn, cannibalization, consumer sentiment — with probability intervals
- **Competitor response map**: who will answer with price, who with promo, who with a media attack — and when

## How it's implemented (technology)

### Data and calibration
- Panel and sales data, social listening, search demand, promo history
- Calibration of consumer segment profiles (silicon sample, Pillar 3)

### Behavioral core
- Agent-based model of consumer choice: brand switching, price and promo sensitivity, loyalty
- Modeling of cannibalization within your own product line
- Diffusion of the reaction: who notices first, how the reaction spreads across channels

### Competitor models
- Behavioral models of key competitors based on their historical moves (price wars, promo calendars, media attacks)
- Forecast of response time and strength for each scenario (linked to market war games, Product 24)

### Scenario engine
- 100+ simulations with variable conditions → probability corridors for each scenario
- Scenario ranking: revenue × risk × reputational effects
- Recommendations on move parameters: price, timing, communication

## Implementation procedure

1. **Weeks 1–2. Data and context** — data inventory, competitive landscape, decision objectives; model calibration
2. **Week 3. Baseline forecast and scenarios** — reaction corridors, competitor response map, scenario ranking; strategy session
3. **After launch** — monitoring actuals vs. forecast, recalculating scenarios, adjusting the move

## FAQ

**Q: How is this different from classic demand forecasting?**
A: Classic forecasting extrapolates time series. We model behavior: customers switch, competitors respond, sentiment influences demand. That is why the model sees trend breaks that time series cannot describe.

**Q: What if we don't have panel data?**
A: The model is calibrated on open signals (shelf prices, search demand, social listening) and your sales data of any depth. Uncertainty zones are flagged; the value of scenario comparison is preserved.

**Q: How accurate are the corridors?**
A: In retrospective cases (past launches and price changes), the corridors cover actuals in the vast majority of scenarios; accuracy depends on data depth and is honestly presented before the decision is made.

**Q: Is this applicable to B2B?**
A: Yes, with adaptation: instead of thousands of customers, we model key accounts and tender behavior. The mechanics are the same; the population scale is different.
